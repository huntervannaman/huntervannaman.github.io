Hi! My name is Hunter Vannaman, a research and data analysis professional who successfully defended their thesis and graduated with a Master of Science in Psychological Science in 2024.

I believe strongly in the use of research- and data-driven decisions to bring real-world benefits to students and communities in need. In accordance with these beliefs, I focused my time in academia exploring practically useful research topics in my thesis and my coursework. As a result of both my coursework and teaching, I have a wide breadth of experience in research and survey design, as well as appropriate data cleaning, manipulation, and analysis methodologies.

Since my graduation, I have focused my efforts on further expanding my research and data analysis skillsets, developing my data cleaning and statistical analysis skills with R and exploring business intelligence and data management with Power BI and SQL.

Research Experience
======
During undergrad, I centered my coursework heavily on experimental psychology and learning the tools used in psychology research. I enjoyed working closely with faculty members in the design, conduction, and communication of research, specifically in the areas of cognitive and environmental psychology.

My graduate school coursework further exposed me to the wide variety of perspectives and research methodologies used in the different disciplines of psychology, with a great underlying emphasis on research design and data analysis - the core principles behind conducting structured, useful research. I completed several research proposals and presentations for courses, as well as worked alongside faculty members on research projects in the areas of math anxiety and metacognition.

My [master's thesis](https://digitalcommons.unf.edu/etd/1267/) explored the mechanisms behind the beneficial effects of testing on learning, with an emphasis on the role that making metacognitive judgments plays in these effects.

Teaching Experience
======
During graduate school, I was selected for a merit-based teaching assistantship in which I was the instructor of record for four sections of a Research Methods in Psychology lab course. This course instilled in undergraduate students the fundamentals of APA style, psychological research design, science comprehension, and the basics of data cleaning and analysis using the IBM SPSS statistical package.

As instructor of record, I was solely responsible for conducting the class, including lecturing, grading, accommodating struggling and disabled students, and creating supplemental materials.

My teaching philosophy is to meet students where they are; I seek to cultivate a casual and understanding environment to ensure that students feel comfortable asking questions and nobody feels lost.
